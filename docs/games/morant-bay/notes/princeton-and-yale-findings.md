# What the Princeton and Yale pages gave us

The owner opened the pages in a browser and sent screenshots (not kept in the repo). The photographs were cropped from them into `images/restricted/`. They are thumbnails from web pages, so they are small. The full images are on the Princeton and Yale sites.

## 1. Princeton Graphic Arts, "Photography album documenting the Morant Bay Rebellion, Jamaica 1865, and more" (Julie L. Melby, 5 October 2009)

https://www.princeton.edu/~graphicarts/2009/10/photography_album_documenting.html

- An album of **165 albumen photographs**: 59 of the Morant Bay rebellion (1865), 32 of the Hazara campaign (1867 to 1870), 64 of Malta, Ireland, Guernsey and elsewhere. Mostly unidentified amateurs; some by Samuel Bourne, G. Sommer, William Lawrence and Francis Frith.
- The careful notes suggest the compiler may be **Alexander Dudgeon Gulland, MD**, a staff assistant surgeon of the 6th Foot, which was in Jamaica in 1865 ("may be", the post says).
- What the post shows: **"Colonel Fyfe"** with two Maroons; **"Maroons"** in leaf camouflage; **"Mr Price, Murdered"**; portraits captioned **Mr Eyre** and **Brig. Gen. Nelson** (the captions mention the Jamaica Committee and acquittal); a page of "The Victims of the Jamaica Rebellion of 1865" portraits; four villager cabinet cards; a view collage; and the big tree. The post says there is also **a portrait of George William Gordon** (not shown in the post).
- It says the portraits were probably gathered after the rebellion and some may come from the only commercial studio in Jamaica then, run by Adolphe Duperly (1801 to 1865), taken over by his son Armond.
- Dr Mimi Sheller (Drexel) contributed a summary of events (below). In the comments she wrote that the accompanying text "describing the events at Morant Bay is slightly inaccurate". Treat the post's own narrative with care.
- A commenter notes many of these photographs were published in **an illustrated booklet printed in Kingston in 1965 for the centenary**, long out of print. That booklet is a lead (the NLJ may hold it).
- References listed: *Harper's Weekly*, 23 December 1865, "The Town of Morant"; *Illustrated London News*, 25 November 1865, "The Scene of the Negro Insurrection"; Heuman, *The Killing Time* (1994); Erickson, "Empire or Anarchy" (*Journal of Negro History*, April 1959); Bleby, *The Reign of Terror* (1868).

### From Dr Sheller's summary and Heuman, as quoted in the post (secondary)

- Heuman (1994, p.xiii), as quoted: "by the end of the day the crowd had killed eighteen people and wounded thirty-one others. **Seven members of the crowd died.** In the days which followed, bands of people in different parts of the parish killed two planters and threatened the lives of many others." This confirms my reading that the "25" at Morant Bay is 18 plus 7.
- The crowd "was said to have been led by Mrs. **Letitia Geoghagan**, the mother of James, Isabella, and Charles; both she and one of her sons were executed following the imposition of martial law". Isabella Geoghagan challenged the police on 7 October. Sheller says Bogle and some followers from Stony Gut rescued James outside the court (the Commission says "bystanders").
- She quotes the Royal Commission evidence for the arrival. **I checked it in the scan: it is Part II, pdf page 247, Questions 9069 to 9098**, a Volunteer's evidence:
  - Q 9072: "Afterwards we saw them coming round the corner into the Parade, with guns and bayonets fixed, sticks, swords, machettes, and lances; they were advancing, **blowing shells and playing drums**."
  - Q 9074 to 9078: "a woman named Geoghagan" (now dead) "first fired a stone, and several other women followed her, and then the men rushed right in". She was "just in front of us, in the north part of the Parade". Q 9079: "She was the guide, and led them; she came up in front of them." Q 9080: "She was a sort of leader." (Verified in the scan, pdf p.247.)
  - Q 9077: the Custos was reading and calling out "Peace!"; other gentlemen called "Peace in Her Majesty's name!" "But the people were saying, '**No peace! Hell to-day!**'"
  - Q 9097: on the Saturday (7 October) he heard drums and saw men marching from the Parade "with drum and fife", armed with sticks, with shouldered arms.
- This settles the drums: **confirmed for the 7 October crowd and for the 11 October arrival**, from the Commission's own evidence. It also shows women leading the crowd, and the first stone thrown by a woman, on one witness's word. Letitia Geoghegan was a real person: no invented words, and her part is told as one witness's account, with the rest of the evidence checked first.

## 2. Princeton, "Jamaica Rebellion broadside" (2015)

https://graphicarts.princeton.edu/2015/02/04/jamaica-rebellion-broadside

- A huge poster, **"GORDON AND EYRE"**, 139 by 109 cm, issued in 1866 by the Committee of the British and Foreign Anti-Slavery Society, printed by E. C. Osborne, Birmingham.
- It quotes **Eyre's January 1866 despatch to Cardwell**, which calls Gordon "universally regarded as a bad man in every sense of the word" and says he was "reported to be grossly immoral and an adulterer, a liar, a swindler, dishonest, cruel, vindictive, and a hypocrite". Then a protest signed by people who had lived in the island many years, including James M. Phillippo (Senior Baptist Missionary) and "116 other persons", declaring the claims "utterly without foundation".
- Princeton also says the Bank of Jamaica issued a new $10 note with Gordon in **1976**, and that the *New York Daily Tribune* of 26 December 1865 headlined "George William Gordon, the Jamaica Martyr".
- **Use:** a real document pair for an on-screen card (Eyre's accusation and the protest). Check the despatch's wording against the Blue Book before it goes in the game.

## 3. Yale Beinecke, album page 72 (item 15817490)

https://collections.library.yale.edu/catalog/15817490

- Collection: William Walker Whitehall Johnston, 1835 to 1886 (GEN MSS 887, Box 1, folder 70), "circa 1865". The catalogue's title describes the **verso**: "Tree at crossroads near Morant Bay, Jamaica; rebels during the Morant Bay Rebellion assembled by this tree on October 12, 1865, immediately before attacking the Court House".
- The **verso** photograph is the same silk-cotton tree photograph as in the Princeton album, so it was a circulated picture. The date in the caption (12 October) conflicts with the Commission (the courthouse attack was on 11 October; on the 12th they went to Coley and Bath). Treat the date as unreliable. The tree is a good landmark; whether it is where the Torrington party joined the Stony Gut party is not known.
- The **recto** carries two photographs that the catalogue does not describe:
  - **Upper:** Morant Bay seen over fenced open ground, mountains behind. A long masonry building with a tiled hip roof, a balustraded parapet, a loggia of six square columns and arched end windows stands at the centre. Wooden two-storey houses with galleries and a hipped house stand beside it.
  - **Lower:** a view across a fenced garden to a two-storey house with a shingle roof, and behind it a **roofless masonry shell with square window openings**, apparently burnt out.
- **Not confirmed:** whether the upper building is the courthouse before the fire, and whether the lower shell is the burnt courthouse. The ILN engraving of the ruin (long, with a row of round arches) and the NLJ print (a two-storey shell with a central arch) do not obviously match either. Next: the neighbouring album pages (71 and 73) and the Yale folder may caption them.

## What this adds to the game's look (rule 3)

Real 1865 photographs now exist for: the town's streets and houses, a large masonry public building, a burnt shell, the crossroads tree, villagers' everyday dress (head wraps, white cotton, baskets on heads, straw hats), a gentleman's frock coat and top hat, Colonel Fyfe's dress, and Maroons in leaf camouflage. None of them is a texture: build from them.
