# The girl and her family: my recommendation (for the owner to confirm)

The owner asked "what do you think" on 23 November 2026. This is Claude's view, with the reasons, for you to confirm or change.

## Who she is

**Hannah** (a first name that fits the time and is not the name of any real person in the Commission's evidence; the surname to choose, for example Reid). Fifteen. Born free in 1850, twelve years after full freedom. She learned to read at the chapel school, so she is the one her neighbours ask to read letters and notices. She sells provisions at Morant Bay market with her mother. Fictional, and her journal page says so.

**Why born free matters:** her mother was enslaved as a child and her grandmother until freedom. Rumours that the planters meant to bring slavery back were real in 1865 (the Morant Bay article on Wikipedia records them). The fear in the game is double: of losing freedom, and then of the soldiers. The 27 years of "obedience to the laws of our Queen" in Bogle's own letter (RC p.14) are the years her family has lived free.

## The family (five bodies, kept small)

| Who | Age | Role in the story | Real anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mother** | about 35 | A higgler at the market. Cannot read; proud that Hannah can. Practical, afraid, holds the family together. Hides with the children in chapter 5. | The market-day crowd on 7 October; the provision sellers |
| **Gran** | about 65 | Remembers slavery. Voices the old fear that freedom can be taken back. | The rumours of 1865; the 1834 and 1838 freedom |
| **Father** | about 40 | A small settler on a few acres at Stony Gut, a member of Bogle's chapel. Afraid of losing the land ("the Queen's land"). **Pressed to go** on 10 and 11 October. | The Commission says the originators were mostly small "free settlers" (RC p.18); witnesses say men were threatened and sworn: "if you won't go, we will take off your heads" (Part II, pdf p.156, Qs 4189-4198) |
| **Her little brother** | 6 | The reason she must keep quiet when hiding. | Fear in the cane and the cave (Voices) |

No older brother: it keeps the cast to four bodies besides Hannah, and a father who is pressed to go is truer to the evidence than a willing marcher.

## What happens to her father (my recommendation)

He is **taken** by soldiers on the night of 19 October from the hiding place near the burnt chapel, walked away at lantern light toward Morant Bay with other men. **The game does not say what happened to him.** The story ends before any court. The last chapter says plainly: "Hannah and her family are made up. The Commission named 439 people who were shot or hanged, and found that many of the dead 'were neither directly nor indirectly parties to the disturbances.'" The record names no individual that Hannah could be, so the game does not decide.

Why: it is honest (we cannot know), it keeps the fear after the story ends, and it does not make a fictional family the exception that is saved.

## The names question: yes, with limits

**Recommendation: yes, she writes names, three times, and the meaning of the act changes each time.**

1. **Chapter 1, hope.** The Stony Gut people wrote to Gordon in July 1862 asking for £150 to pay the tax that would let them be **registered as voters** (RC p.29). Hannah writes the list of names for the registration. Writing names means a vote.
2. **Chapter 3, fear.** At a night meeting at the chapel someone puts the pen in her hand again, for a list of names. She understands only halfway what it is. The Commission found that at meetings an oath was administered, **names were taken down** and a pledge of secrecy required, and that "the terms of the oath were not shown" (RC p.12). The scene stops before any oath is spoken. She is a reader the adults use, not a recruiter.
3. **Chapter 6, grief.** The Commission's returns name the dead. The closing reads some of those real names, with care, as the list the Commission made.

**The guardrails:**
- She is **pressed**, not willing, and never does harm. She is a child with a skill.
- **The game never says the soldiers found the list.** We do not know that. The fear is hers, not a claim.
- The oath words are **not shown or invented**. Only "cleave to the black" is recorded, and it was said to the police.
- The Commission's own conclusions are told fairly: a planned resistance, and punishments far beyond what was needed (RC p.40-41). The game does not excuse the killings and does not excuse the reprisals.

**What could go wrong:** some players will feel the game blames a child. The answer is in the sources: the Commission itself records men and women forced to swear under threat of death (Part II, pdf p.156, Qs 4189-4198). If you would rather she not write the oath list, the fallback is that she **only reads** (the Queen's Advice, the Underhill letter, Bogle's letter on the 10th) and is asked to copy the 10 October letter. That is weaker: it loses the names thread.

## For the owner to confirm

1. Hannah, aged 15, and the four-body family.
2. The father is taken and his fate is not told.
3. Yes to the three name lists, with the guardrails.
