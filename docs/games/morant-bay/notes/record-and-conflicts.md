# The record, and where the sources disagree

Working notes, not committed. Printed page numbers are the Royal Commission report's (printed page = pdf page minus 7). "RC" = Royal Commission report, Part I, 9 April 1866 (signed Storks, Gurney, Maule).

## What the Commission itself says (the strongest source)

| Date and event | RC page |
|---|---|
| Sat 7 Oct 1865, market day. Geoghegan told a boy to pay the 4s fine and not the 12s 6d costs; rescued twice; police beaten. Lewis Miller's land case from Stony Gut heard the same day (the people said the land was "the Queen's"). | 10-11 |
| Mon 9 Oct: warrants for two Bogles and others. A meeting with an oath at Coley that night. | 11-12 |
| Tue 10 Oct, early: six police and two rural constables to Stony Gut (about five miles from Morant Bay). Bogle had the warrant read, refused, cried "Help, here". Captain Grant: "Turn out, men". 300 to 500 men from the chapel and a cane field. Three police held for hours, released after swearing to "cleave to the black". Bogle said they had expected to go to Morant Bay that day but it was late; on the morrow there was a vestry. | 11 |
| Drilling: about 300 men in three companies under "Colonel" Bowie, Moses Bogle and James Dacres, about three quarters of an hour, "with drum and shells". Parties with fife and drum on the 10th. | 13 |
| The letter signed by Bogle and 19 others, 10 Oct after noon, delivered at Spanish Town (about 50 miles) 10 to 11 am on the 11th. The Commission calls it "scarcely concealed defiance". | 14 |
| Wed 11 Oct: vestry about noon; between three and four a crowd arrives in parties; about 100 from Torrington join at the entrance. They take the police station's arms (muskets without flints). | 11, 13 |
| "Peace, peace" answered by "War". Riot Act begun. Stones thrown (the Commission finds before the firing, apparently by women and others at the side). Captain Hitchins struck on the forehead, orders fire with the Custos's authority. | 11-12 |
| The school-house burned, the fire spread to the courthouse. Dark. 18 killed, 31 wounded. 51 prisoners freed. The magazine (about 300 arms) held. Bogle is quoted by witnesses: "Let us put fire upon the Court House". | 12 |
| A Black vestryman, Mr Price, beaten to death ("a black skin but a white heart"). A service in Bogle's chapel after, giving thanks. | 14 |
| 12 Oct: 200 men with shells blowing to Coley; Bath taken, "drums beating"; the Maroons' horn drives them out. The Maroons Hayfield party holds Bath from 13 Oct. | 15, 25 |
| Fri 13 Oct: martial law proclaimed at Head Quarter House, Kingston, for the county of Surrey except Kingston; Council of War of about 30. | 18-19 |
| 18 Oct: 90 men take Stony Gut. 19 Oct: the chapel and about eight cottages burned. | 20 |
| 19 Oct: 50 Maroons under Colonel Fyfe march through Moortown "to which place they belonged". Sat 21 Oct: Torrington attacked (7 shot). They shot at most 25 in all; about 141 cottages burned. Nelson's orders: never molest a woman or child; do not shoot a man who surrenders. | 25 |
| Mon 23 Oct: Bogle "apprehended with his few remaining followers"; 24th conveyed to Morant Bay. (By whom: not stated.) | 39 |
| Gordon: trial of six hours, five witnesses for the prosecution, one for the defence; evidence "wholly insufficient"; Nelson confirmed 21 Oct; Eyre agreed 22 Oct; executed 7.10 am Mon 23 Oct. | 37-38 |
| 354 executed by courts-martial, 85 killed without trial: 439. 1,000 houses burned. At least 600 flogged (wires in the cords at Bath). | 25-26 |
| Conclusions: a planned resistance; land free of rent the main aim; martial law too long; death sentences too frequent; floggings reckless and at Bath barbarous; burning "wanton and cruel". Eyre praised for the early days. | 40-41 |

## One more caution

The NLJ leaflet (a later heritage text printed beside the photograph in image 7) is not a primary source and disagrees with the Commission in several places. Treat it as a record of how the story is told in Jamaica, not as evidence.

## Added 23 November 2026: from the Commission's evidence (Part II) and the Princeton post

| Date and event | Source |
|---|---|
| Sat 7 Oct: men marched from the Parade "with drum and fife", armed with sticks and shouldered arms; Captain Grant formed them four deep and Bogle was "in the rear of Grant". | RC Part II, pdf p.247-248, Qs 9097-9100 (a Volunteer) |
| Wed 11 Oct, the arrival: the crowd came round the corner into the Parade "with guns and bayonets fixed, sticks, swords, machettes, and lances; ... blowing shells and playing drums"; the lane was crowded. | RC Part II, pdf p.247, Qs 9072-9073 |
| A woman named Geoghagan (dead by 1866) "first fired a stone, and several other women followed her, and then the men rushed right in"; "She was the guide, and led them; she came up in front of them"; "She was a sort of leader." One witness's account. | RC Part II, pdf p.247, Qs 9074-9080 |
| The Custos was reading and calling "Peace!"; other gentlemen called "Peace in Her Majesty's name!"; the people said "No peace! Hell to-day!" | RC Part II, pdf p.247, Q 9077 |
| Mrs Letitia Geoghagan, mother of James, Isabella and Charles, said to have led the crowd; she and one son were executed under martial law. | Sheller, in the Princeton post (secondary; check in Part II) |
| 18 killed and 31 wounded by the crowd; **seven of the crowd died**; later two planters were killed. | Heuman 1994, p.xiii, as quoted in the Princeton post (secondary) |

Resolved by this: the drums at the arrival (confirmed), the "25 at Morant Bay" (18 plus 7).

New conflicts:
- The **Yale album caption** gives 12 October for the rebels assembling by the tree "immediately before attacking the Court House". The Commission has the attack on 11 October (RC p.11-12).
- **Who rescued Geoghegan** on 7 October: bystanders (RC p.10), or Bogle and Stony Gut followers (Sheller, in the Princeton post). Dr Sheller also wrote that the post's accompanying text is "slightly inaccurate".
- Eyre's January 1866 despatch calls Gordon a "bad man in every sense"; 116+ people protested it as "utterly without foundation" (the 1866 broadside).
