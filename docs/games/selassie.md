# The Emperor's Arrival

A six-chapter game about Haile Selassie I's arrival at Palisadoes Airport on 21 April 1966. The title follows the owner's chosen spelling. Folder and save namespace: `selassie`.

## People rebuilt (16 November 2026)

All fifteen recipes were rebuilt from main with the fixed shared builder (see `docs/HANDOFF.md`, "People pass"); draft PR #108's rejected bodies were not used. Each recipe names its own face shape. The Rastafari men have their own builds, beards (`beard` in the recipe) and locs that grow from the whole scalp, lie on the head and fall back. Crowd and phone copies keep eyes, brows and lips, use fewer and thicker locs, and have a hair-coloured scalp so a simplified cap never shows a bald patch. Haile Selassie I: a dark beard round the mouth and along the jaw, greying at the chin, a narrower face and a cap that sits at the brow, after the owner's April 1966 photographs. It is still a stylised likeness. The police cap sits lower and the tunic's collar no longer shows bare neck. ## Story and play

The player is Ruth, a fictional child aged 10. Grandma Adina, Mum, Uncle Levi, Mr. Brown, Teddy and Miss Pearl are fictional. Young Adina in the short 1930 memory is also fictional. None of their words are historical quotations.

1. **The picture in the tin** (place `yard`, then `memory`). The family yard in Kingston, April 1966, opens a playable memory of young Adina hearing about the 1930 coronation. Carry a newspaper to a map and ask the vendor about Ethiopia.
2. **Everyone has a reason** (place `downtown`, 20 April 1966, the day before). A shopping street downtown, near the Parade, getting ready. Listen to Mum (at the dressmaker's where she sewed little flags) and Uncle Levi (with a handcart of palm fronds) in either order. Carry the bundle of flags to Levi's cart, buy a Gleaner from the newsboy and take it to Grandma on her bench at the Parade, then get water at the standpipe, bread from the bakery table and oranges from the higgler and put them by the bag.
3. **The road to Palisadoes** (places `parade`, then `road`, 21 April 1966). Mum leads through the Parade bus park (shared guide system). Buy the tickets from the conductor's wooden rack, give them to Mum, and climb on the bus. The bus stops in the traffic on the Palisadoes road, so the family walks the last part with the crowd: bring Grandma water, look over the harbour to Kingston and the mountains, and walk on to the airport and the family meeting post.
4. **Before the plane** (place `airport`). Hear the family, spread a cloth, carry water and share it with two neighbours. Uncle Levi describes his own faith and unfair treatment.
5. **The arrival** (place `airport`). Choose to watch the cinematic or read the event. A four-engine DC-6 arrives. Mortimo Planno and Haile Selassie I appear at the steps. Neither has invented dialogue. Choose a personal memory after speaking with Grandma.
6. **What we carry home** (place `lane`, that evening). The family's lane at dusk: lit windows and street lamps, neighbours round the corner shop's radio, children playing. Mum has left the bus ticket on the veranda rail; put it beside the picture on the veranda table, listen to the family, and keep a journal memory.

**The places (review of 16 November 2026).** The owner found the visit's scenes "boring and repetitive" and the family yard reused (and looking like Windrush's). The yard is now used only in chapter 1 and is dressed as a Kingston yard of 1966: zinc fences, a plastered house with glass louvre windows and a narrow veranda, a Jamaican flag on the house, a radio on the veranda rail, pots in kerosene tins. About 28 background people: shoppers in the piazzas, people walking the street, talk at shop doors, listeners at the record shop door, the newsboy, baker, higgler and pharmacist at their pitches, and children by the Parade.

## Historical basis

- **2 November 1930:** Haile Selassie I was crowned Emperor of Ethiopia. Rastafari emerged in Jamaica in the 1930s. The memory, newspaper and keepsake illustration are reconstructed, not reproduced archive objects. Source: Oxford University Press, *The emergence of Rastafari*: https://academic.oup.com/book/7074/chapter-abstract/151570898
- **Getting ready (checked 16 November 2026).** The Gleaner's 1966 report (republished 30 October 2024, https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20241030/haile-selassie-i-cries-pandemonium-erupts-palisadoes-airport) says Thursday 21 April 1966 was a public holiday, that people started to gather at Palisadoes on Wednesday, April 20, 1966, and came "on foot, in cars, in drays, in carts, in trucks, in hired buses (including JOS buses), by bicycles"; the crowd waved palm fronds and red, green and gold flags, sang, drummed and blew abengs; it rained about an hour before the descent. The Gleaner's "Pieces of the Past" feature (https://old.jamaica-gleaner.com/pages/history/story0022.html) adds a 5 p.m. civic reception at the National Stadium that day, a state dinner at King's House, a sitting of Parliament and an honorary Doctor of Laws from the University of the West Indies. The Gleaner's photo flashback (http://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20160420/photo-flashback-visit-emperor-haile-selassie-i-jamaica-1966) captions "Flags, robes, regalia and the smiling faces of welcome along the royal route" and says he addressed both houses of Parliament at Gordon House. David Katz (Red Bull Music Academy, https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2014/02/haile-selassie-in-jamaica/) writes that thousands more lined the roadside from the airport into Kingston, some with flags, and that Rastafarians brought drums and banners. **Not confirmed:** official bunting or decorated streets downtown. So the game shows no city-wide decorations: only a few flags out from shops, pennants at the fictional dressmaker's, Mum's home-sewn flags and Levi's palm fronds. Mum sewing flags and the shops are fictional; the journal says so.
- **Buses (checked 16 November 2026).** The Jamaica Omnibus Service took over Kingston's buses on 15 December 1953 with single-deck Leylands; a 1954 report (Commercial Motor, https://archive.commercialmotor.com/article/26th-february-1954/137/order-out-of-chaos-bus-operation) says about 15 services radiate from Parade Gardens. A Gleaner feature (https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/art-leisure/20180218/jolly-joseph-kingston) remembers the buses as "metallic grey and dark green, but the colours changed over time" and a conductor "plucking those tickets from those wooden racks". The bus in the game is a general picture, not one make; its blinds read PALISADOES and SPECIAL (made up for the picture).
- **The Daily Gleaner** in the game shows only its name and the date, Wednesday, April 20, 1966; no headline is invented.
- **Radio:** RJR began in 1950 and JBC radio in 1959 (JBC television in 1963). The radios in the lane play no recorded words.
- **20 April 1966:** people were already gathering before the arrival day. The fictional family journey does not claim a recorded route or bus timetable. Source: The Gleaner's retrospective: https://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/lead-stories/20160421/online-special-when-selassie-came-looking-back-visit-emperor-50-years
- **21 April 1966:** arrival at Palisadoes Airport, Kingston, during a three-day visit. Use the period airport name, not its later name, Norman Manley International Airport. Crowd estimates vary; the game says many thousands. Sources: National Library of Jamaica https://nljdigital.nlj.gov.jm/items/show/7914 and UWI Museum eyewitness recollections https://uwimuseum.wordpress.com/2016/05/04/video-selassie-recollections/
- Photographs show spectators on a terminal roof. The terminal massing, tower, roof spectators and apron are a general reconstruction, not a surveyed copy. Source: National Library of Jamaica https://nljdigital.nlj.gov.jm/items/show/824
- **The aircraft (checked again 16 November 2026).** LIFE's editor's note to Lynn Pelham's photographs calls it "the emperor's DC-6" (https://time.com/3880376/haile-selassie-in-jamaica-photos-from-a-rastafari-milestone/). The Gleaner's 1966 report says he "stood on the steps of an aircraft of Ethiopian airliner which had brought him from Trinidad and Tobago" and describes "the insignia of a roaring lion on the front" (https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20241030/haile-selassie-i-cries-pandemonium-erupts-palisadoes-airport). Ethiopian Air Lines bought Douglas DC-6Bs from 1956/58; in the 1960s they were registered ET-AAX, ET-AAY and ET-AAZ (Ken Fielding's Air Transport Archive, ET-AAY at Frankfurt, 8 December 1967: https://www.flickr.com/photos/kenfielding/5574946065). The airline became a share company and took the name Ethiopian Airlines in 1965.
  - **Confirmed and modelled:** a four-engine Douglas DC-6 type of Ethiopian's, at Palisadoes, with the passenger door at the back on the left (port) side and boarding steps rolled to it, as in the arrival photographs. The model is built to the DC-6B's published size: about 32.2 m long, 35.8 m span, 8.7 m to the top of the fin, a 3.3 m fuselage, four radial engines with 4 m three-blade propellers, tricycle gear (`games/selassie/src/aircraft.js`).
  - **The tail (owner's note "the wing of the tail is off", 16 November 2026).** Rebuilt to the DC-6/DC-6B layout: the tail cone sweeps up behind the rear door (the underside rises, the top line stays nearly level) instead of narrowing symmetrically; the horizontal tail is about 11.6 m across (it was 13.4 m), flat with no dihedral, a straight tapered planform with a little sweep on the leading edge and rounded tips, set low on the tail cone on its centre line (before, it sat at the top of the fuselage and reached past the end of the tail); the fin is one outline with a long dorsal fillet rising from the top of the fuselage into a straight swept leading edge, a rounded tip 8.74 m above the ground (it was square) and a nearly upright rudder down to the end of the tail cone, with a white tail light. The three tail bands keep their general reconstruction and are refitted to the new fin. Views: `?shot=plane&view=side|rear|tail|rear3q|top`. The head check's camera test follows the swept-up tail.
  - **Not confirmed, so kept general:** which of the three DC-6 aircraft flew that day (no registration is assigned in the model), the exact paint scheme (the gold cheatline with red and green lines, the white top and the tail bands in the Ethiopian colours are a general reconstruction from the owner's reference photographs; the tail colours follow the owner's later twin-engine reference) and the exact lion badge (a stylised lion's head in a ring on the nose, standing for the lion the Gleaner describes, not a copy of the real badge). The owner requested the complete painted title "ETHIOPIAN AIRLINES" on 16 November 2026. It is shown on both sides. The name is period-correct: the airline was renamed in 1965 ([company history](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/ethiopian-airlines)). This expands the "ETHIOPIAN" lettering visible in the Jamaica arrival photographs at the owner's request. No "White Nile" name is added: the [ET-AAH record](https://airport-data.com/aircraft/ET-AAH) identifies that name with a Boeing 720, while [LIFE's Jamaica gallery](https://www.life.com/people/haile-selassie-in-jamaica-photos-from-a-rastafari-milestone/) identifies the arrival plane as a DC-6. The owner chose to remove the unverified name after that distinction was checked online. The [NLJ arrival record D0008807](https://nljdigital.nlj.gov.jm/items/show/7914) also documents the arrival scene. The uploaded reference photographs remain unchanged.
  - The arrival photograph record: https://www.gettyimages.co.uk/detail/news-photo/haile-selassie-i-emperor-of-ethiopia-arrives-at-palisadoes-news-photo/75572629 (not readable from this machine; not used for any detail above).
- The Gleaner's report also says it had rained about an hour before he came down, and that he waved and was moved to tears at the top of the steps. The cinematic shows the salute from the owner's reference and clip and a wave at the foot of the steps; it does not show tears or rain.
- **Mortimo Planno:** a real Rastafari elder who helped the welcome proceed at the aircraft. His name also appears as Mortimer Planno. He is shown silently. The Gleaner retrospective and arrival photographs document his role.
- Rastafari beliefs are diverse. Many regard Haile Selassie I as divine. This is presented as faith, not a historical proof of divinity or a universal Jamaican belief. No miracle is presented as fact.
- **1963 / 2017:** the journal mentions Coral Gardens and the Government's apology in 2017 for state-inflicted violence. No violence is reenacted. Source: https://jis.gov.jm/government-apologises-victims-coral-gardens/
- **Grounation Day:** the anniversary remembers the visit. It is not the Emperor's birthday. Source: https://jis.gov.jm/minister-grange-celebrates-rastafari-groundation-day/

## Characters and graphics

All bodies have game-specific `sel_` recipes. Children use child shapes, build upscale 1.26 and runtime scale 0.8. Skin swatches remain in the established range and full bodies use the shared 1.55 runtime skin multiplier. Crowd copies bake that multiplier into skin vertex colours before joining. Hair is not brightened.

The airport includes distinct Rastafari elder, younger man, woman and knitted-tam recipes, alongside other visitors. Locs, beards, plain period clothes, striped tams and a headwrap vary the crowd. Greeters carry Ethiopian-colour flags above their heads or palm fronds. Ceremonial police wear red tunics, white belts and checked black caps, with ceremonial rifles. Roof spectators, apron markings, glazed terminal bays, an airport tower, baggage carts and wet-apron patches follow the photographic setting within a compressed reconstruction. Rastafari identity is not reduced to one costume or hair style.

Close figures retain separate garments and the full skeleton. Crowd copies join painted garments and skin before packing, while preserving named bone weights and the original eyes. They use the shared animation and grounding systems. Phones use fewer ground and roof spectators, fewer full animation updates and capped pixel ratio. Static terminal and aircraft pieces are batched; propellers, flags and people remain movable. Contact shadows use one instanced batch per place.

Selassie's uniform has a khaki tunic and lapels, narrow pale shirt opening and tie, red gorget tabs, small multicolour ribbon bars, flat service stars, Sam Browne belt and shoulder strap, fine gold cords, khaki shoulder boards and a low peaked cap. The shoulder strap's bone weights are smoothed along its length, so its edge no longer juts where it crosses the shoulder. He salutes at the top of the rear port steps with the owner's clip (below).

## The arrival cinematic (review of 16 November 2026)

`games/selassie/src/director.js`. The DC-6 lands on the runway heading west, rolls out, turns up the taxiway and onto the apron line, and stops with its rear door at the steps, nose west, port side to the crowd and the terminal. Shots: the crowd by the terminal hearing it and turning to the sky; touchdown beside the runway; the taxi in, over the shoulders of the western crowd; a wide view of the welcome and the whole aircraft; the steps rolled to the door and the door swinging open; Mortimo Planno at the foot of the steps, calming the crowd (no words); Haile Selassie I at the top, saluting; the crowd's faces seen from the foot of the steps; him at the foot of the steps beside Planno, waving; and Grandma. Every camera is outside the aircraft, the steps, every person and every held flag or frond, never under the ground and never on an empty view for more than 2 seconds; the head check tests this along the whole cinematic. The aircraft's wing and propellers sweep the apron east of the steps as it taxis, so the welcome there comes forward only once it has stopped (shown at a cut), as the crowd surged forward after it landed.

**The owner's clips.** `shared/anim-sources/owner/Salute_selassie.fbx` is retargeted with the shared retargeter and packed with the shared clean-up into this game's own `games/selassie/site/models/anims_selassie.js` (`SaluteHS`; `tools/selassie/clips.json`, `tools/selassie/pack_clips.mjs`), loaded after the shared library, as Tivoli does; the shared library is not rebuilt. On his body the hand comes up to the brim of the cap and does not pass through the head or cap (salute screenshots and the cloth check, 0). Its right hand is held flat as it starts, because a curl of the fingers pinched the palm as the arm came down.

`shared/anim-sources/owner/Descending_Stairs.fbx` was retargeted and measured on his body and **does not fit**: it is one step down that does not loop (the first and last poses differ by up to 93 degrees at the knees and ankles, with both feet together at each end), and on his longer legs the standing foot sinks 10 to 20 cm into the step; with that foot held still, each step carries him only about 4 to 9 cm down for 40 cm or more forward, nothing like aircraft steps. Played step after step it would show feet sliding or sinking, so it is not shipped. His walk down is not shown step by step: the camera turns to the crowd, then finds him at the foot of the steps. A clip made on a body like his, or a hand-made step-down (`tools/anims/handmade.py`), would let the walk down be shown.

**The crowd.** About 130 people on the apron are placed at random (a fixed seed) at least 0.8 m apart, never in rows, denser near the steps and clear of the wing, the propellers, the steps and the space at their foot. Each background person gets a body, a height and colours from a hash of their number (`crowdLook` in `cast.js`): children (about 1 in 11) and elders are mixed in everywhere, women and men, four different Rastafari recipes (an elder, a younger man, a woman and a man in a knitted tam) for about half of the airport crowd, and everyday 1960s shirt, dress, trouser and skirt colours. Joined crowd bodies have one painted material, so their clothes are recoloured in their vertex colours (`recolour` in `main.js`), keeping skin, hair, hats and shoes; about 3 in 10 Rastafari visitors keep their recipe's own clothes. People wave, clap, cheer and point at the steps at their own rhythm. Belief is shown as people's own: nothing is presented as a miracle.

**Varied actions, no standing zombies** (owner's note, 16 November 2026; `games/selassie/src/life.js`, `actions`). Every background person in every place gets clips that suit them, from the shared library (`anim-clips.json` now also ships `HappyIdle`, `IdleM`, `OldIdle`, `HeadTurn`, `Agree`, `Nod` and `WaveSlow`): nobody stands in the plain `Idle`.
