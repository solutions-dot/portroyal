# The Emperor's Arrival

A six-chapter game about Haile Selassie I's arrival at Palisadoes Airport on 21 April 1966. The title follows the owner's chosen spelling. Folder and save namespace: `selassie`.

## Story and play

The player is Ruth, a fictional child aged 10. Grandma Adina, Mum, Uncle Levi, Mr. Brown, Teddy and Miss Pearl are fictional. Young Adina in the short 1930 memory is also fictional. None of their words are historical quotations.

1. **The picture in the tin.** Kingston in April 1966 opens a playable memory of young Adina hearing about the 1930 coronation. Carry a newspaper to a map and ask the vendor about Ethiopia.
2. **Everyone has a reason.** Listen to Mum and Uncle Levi in either order. Bring thread to the sewing table, fit a chair slat, and pack water, lunch and the picture.
3. **The road to Palisadoes.** Mum uses the shared guide system. Help Grandma with water, look over the harbour, join the bus and find the family meeting post.
4. **Before the plane.** Hear the family, spread a cloth, carry water and share it with two neighbours. Uncle Levi describes his own faith and unfair treatment.
5. **The arrival.** Choose to watch the cinematic or read the event. A four-engine DC-6 arrives. Mortimo Planno and Haile Selassie I appear at the steps. Neither has invented dialogue. Choose a personal memory after speaking with Grandma.
6. **What we carry home.** Return to the evening yard. Put the bus ticket beside the picture, listen to the family, and keep a journal memory.

Choices change Ruth's remembered experience, not the historical event or whether a belief is correct. There is no sacred-practice minigame, violence or drug-use activity. The crowd scene has a sound toggle, pause and read-instead route. All dialogue is written on screen.

Only current story people receive gold markers. Goals count unordered tasks. The shared arrow appears after 2.5 seconds, or 5 seconds when exploring, and follows unfinished needs. Required objects are visible in the camera carry slot, leave their source and are visibly placed. Shared grips attach neighbours' cups. The journal gains a badge, glow and chime. Chapters, teacher access, native Main Menu links, reset and saved progress follow the established interface. The opening bar disappears once loading finishes.

## Historical basis

- **2 November 1930:** Haile Selassie I was crowned Emperor of Ethiopia. Rastafari emerged in Jamaica in the 1930s. The memory, newspaper and keepsake illustration are reconstructed, not reproduced archive objects. Source: Oxford University Press, *The emergence of Rastafari*: https://academic.oup.com/book/7074/chapter-abstract/151570898
- **20 April 1966:** people were already gathering before the arrival day. The fictional family journey does not claim a recorded route or bus timetable. Source: The Gleaner's retrospective: https://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/lead-stories/20160421/online-special-when-selassie-came-looking-back-visit-emperor-50-years
- **21 April 1966:** arrival at Palisadoes Airport, Kingston, during a three-day visit. Use the period airport name, not its later name, Norman Manley International Airport. Crowd estimates vary; the game says many thousands. Sources: National Library of Jamaica https://nljdigital.nlj.gov.jm/items/show/7914 and UWI Museum eyewitness recollections https://uwimuseum.wordpress.com/2016/05/04/video-selassie-recollections/
- Photographs show spectators on a terminal roof. The terminal massing, tower, roof spectators and apron are a general reconstruction, not a surveyed copy. Source: National Library of Jamaica https://nljdigital.nlj.gov.jm/items/show/824
- **The aircraft (checked again 16 November 2026).** LIFE's editor's note to Lynn Pelham's photographs calls it "the emperor's DC-6" (https://time.com/3880376/haile-selassie-in-jamaica-photos-from-a-rastafari-milestone/). The Gleaner's 1966 report says he "stood on the steps of an aircraft of Ethiopian airliner which had brought him from Trinidad and Tobago" and describes "the insignia of a roaring lion on the front" (https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20241030/haile-selassie-i-cries-pandemonium-erupts-palisadoes-airport). Ethiopian Air Lines bought Douglas DC-6Bs from 1956/58; in the 1960s they were registered ET-AAX, ET-AAY and ET-AAZ (Ken Fielding's Air Transport Archive, ET-AAY at Frankfurt, 8 December 1967: https://www.flickr.com/photos/kenfielding/5574946065). The airline became a share company and took the name Ethiopian Airlines in 1965.
  - **Confirmed and modelled:** a four-engine Douglas DC-6 type of Ethiopian's, at Palisadoes, with the passenger door at the back on the left (port) side and boarding steps rolled to it, as in the arrival photographs. The model is built to the DC-6B's published size: about 32.2 m long, 35.8 m span, 8.7 m to the top of the fin, a 3.3 m fuselage, four radial engines with 4 m three-blade propellers, tricycle gear (`games/selassie/src/aircraft.js`).
  - **Not confirmed, so kept general:** which of the three aircraft flew that day (no registration is shown; the earlier "ET-AAZ" and the made-up name "The White Nile" are removed), the exact paint scheme (the gold cheatline with red and green lines, the white top and the tail bands in the Ethiopian colours are a general reconstruction from the owner's reference photographs; the tail colours follow the owner's later twin-engine reference) and the exact lion badge (a stylised lion's head in a ring on the nose, standing for the lion the Gleaner describes, not a copy of the real badge). The title reads only "ETHIOPIAN", which both the old and the 1965 names begin with.
  - The arrival photograph record: https://www.gettyimages.co.uk/detail/news-photo/haile-selassie-i-emperor-of-ethiopia-arrives-at-palisadoes-news-photo/75572629 (not readable from this machine; not used for any detail above).
- The Gleaner's report also says it had rained about an hour before he came down, and that he waved and was moved to tears at the top of the steps. The cinematic shows the salute from the owner's reference and clip and a wave at the foot of the steps; it does not show tears or rain.
- **Mortimo Planno:** a real Rastafari elder who helped the welcome proceed at the aircraft. His name also appears as Mortimer Planno. He is shown silently. The Gleaner retrospective and arrival photographs document his role.
- Rastafari beliefs are diverse. Many regard Haile Selassie I as divine. This is presented as faith, not a historical proof of divinity or a universal Jamaican belief. No miracle is presented as fact.
- **1963 / 2017:** the journal mentions Coral Gardens and the Government's apology in 2017 for state-inflicted violence. No violence is reenacted. Source: https://jis.gov.jm/government-apologises-victims-coral-gardens/
- **Grounation Day:** the anniversary remembers the visit. It is not the Emperor's birthday. Source: https://jis.gov.jm/minister-grange-celebrates-rastafari-groundation-day/

## Characters and graphics

All bodies have game-specific `sel_` recipes. Children use child shapes, build upscale 1.26 and runtime scale 0.8. Skin swatches remain in the established range and full bodies use the shared 1.55 runtime skin multiplier. Crowd copies bake that multiplier into skin vertex colours before joining. Hair is not brightened.

The airport includes distinct Rastafari elder, younger man, woman and knitted-tam recipes, alongside other visitors. Locs, beards, plain period clothes, striped tams and a headwrap vary the crowd. Greeters carry Ethiopian-colour flags above their heads or palm fronds. Ceremonial police wear red tunics, white belts and checked black caps, with ceremonial rifles. Roof spectators, apron markings, glazed terminal bays, an airport tower, baggage carts and wet-apron patches follow the photographic setting within a compressed reconstruction. Rastafari identity is not reduced to one costume or hair style.

Close figures retain separate garments and the full skeleton. Crowd copies join painted garments and skin before packing, while preserving named bone weights and the original eyes. They use the shared animation and grounding systems. Phones use fewer ground and roof spectators, fewer full animation updates and capped pixel ratio. Static terminal and aircraft pieces are batched; propellers, flags and people remain movable. Contact shadows use one instanced batch per place.

Selassie's uniform has a khaki tunic and lapels, narrow pale shirt opening and tie, red gorget tabs, small multicolour ribbon bars, flat service stars, Sam Browne belt and shoulder strap, fine gold cords, khaki shoulder boards and a low peaked cap. The shoulder strap's bone weights are smoothed along its length, so its edge no longer juts where it crosses the shoulder. He salutes at the top of the rear port steps with the owner's clip (below).

His face (review of 16 November 2026, `tools/selassie/people/sel_emperor66.json`): a narrower, longer oval face, a higher forehead, a longer and narrower nose with a slight hump and a lowered tip, leaner cheeks and a narrow chin. The beard is now the short full beard of the 1960s photographs: over the jaw and the lower cheeks, joined to the moustache at the corners of the mouth, grey at the chin and darker at the sides, taken from the face's own surface so it cannot float (`tools/selassie/imperial_face.py`). Under the chin it stops 3.5 cm behind the chin's tip, in front of the throat, which cleared the "Beard shows through Collar" findings (up to 30 mm). Skin stays at `[151, 103, 75]`, inside the rule 9 range, with the game's 1.55 multiplier. The eyes read narrow under the cap's brim in close-ups, as they did before; a face target that tried to deepen them made them look closed and was taken out. Likeness remains a stylised reconstruction, not a claim of pixel-level identity. Note: `docs/games/selassie-face.jpg` and `selassie-salute.jpg` are game renders for review, not archive photographs.

## The arrival cinematic (review of 16 November 2026)

`games/selassie/src/director.js`. The DC-6 lands on the runway heading west, rolls out, turns up the taxiway and onto the apron line, and stops with its rear door at the steps, nose west, port side to the crowd and the terminal. Shots: the crowd by the terminal hearing it and turning to the sky; touchdown beside the runway; the taxi in, over the shoulders of the western crowd; a wide view of the welcome and the whole aircraft; the steps rolled to the door and the door swinging open; Mortimo Planno at the foot of the steps, calming the crowd (no words); Haile Selassie I at the top, saluting; the crowd's faces seen from the foot of the steps; him at the foot of the steps beside Planno, waving; and Grandma. Every camera is outside the aircraft, the steps, every person and every held flag or frond, never under the ground and never on an empty view for more than 2 seconds; the head check tests this along the whole cinematic. The aircraft's wing and propellers sweep the apron east of the steps as it taxis, so the welcome there comes forward only once it has stopped (shown at a cut), as the crowd surged forward after it landed.

**The owner's clips.** `shared/anim-sources/owner/Salute_selassie.fbx` is retargeted with the shared retargeter and packed with the shared clean-up into this game's own `games/selassie/site/models/anims_selassie.js` (`SaluteHS`; `tools/selassie/clips.json`, `tools/selassie/pack_clips.mjs`), loaded after the shared library, as Tivoli does; the shared library is not rebuilt. On his body the hand comes up to the brim of the cap and does not pass through the head or cap (salute screenshots and the cloth check, 0). Its right hand is held flat as it starts, because a curl of the fingers pinched the palm as the arm came down.

`shared/anim-sources/owner/Descending_Stairs.fbx` was retargeted and measured on his body and **does not fit**: it is one step down that does not loop (the first and last poses differ by up to 93 degrees at the knees and ankles, with both feet together at each end), and on his longer legs the standing foot sinks 10 to 20 cm into the step; with that foot held still, each step carries him only about 4 to 9 cm down for 40 cm or more forward, nothing like aircraft steps. Played step after step it would show feet sliding or sinking, so it is not shipped. His walk down is not shown step by step: the camera turns to the crowd, then finds him at the foot of the steps. A clip made on a body like his, or a hand-made step-down (`tools/anims/handmade.py`), would let the walk down be shown.

**The crowd.** About 130 people on the apron are placed at random (a fixed seed) at least 0.8 m apart, never in rows, denser near the steps and clear of the wing, the propellers, the steps and the space at their foot. Each background person gets a body, a height and colours from a hash of their number (`crowdLook` in `cast.js`): children (about 1 in 11) and elders are mixed in everywhere, women and men, four different Rastafari recipes (an elder, a younger man, a woman and a man in a knitted tam) for about half of the airport crowd, and everyday 1960s shirt, dress, trouser and skirt colours. Joined crowd bodies have one painted material, so their clothes are recoloured in their vertex colours (`recolour` in `main.js`), keeping skin, hair, hats and shoes; about 3 in 10 Rastafari visitors keep their recipe's own clothes. People wave, clap, cheer and point at the steps at their own rhythm. Belief is shown as people's own: nothing is presented as a miracle.

**The title screen** shows the arrival at Palisadoes behind the title instead of the empty yard: the game loads the airport first, poses the arrival at the salute (aircraft at the steps, door open, the welcome around them) and leaves it to live, as the other games' title screens do: the camera drifts slowly, the crowd keeps waving, clapping and turning, and Haile Selassie salutes again now and then. On a phone held upright the camera keeps the steps and him in the middle. The title text keeps its dark backing. Start (or Continue) loads the yard as before.

**More life in every place** (`games/selassie/src/life.js`). The yard has a washing line, a standpipe and bucket, a zinc washtub, a coal pot, crates, a barrel, benches, banana plants by the back fence and flowers, and more neighbours: three pairs chatting with others looking on, two children playing by the washtub and an onlooker by the fence. The 1930 street has a handcart, market and yam baskets, sacks and a crate, a bench and a bunch of bananas, two more conversations, and half its passers-by now wear varied clothes instead of one costume. The road has a fruit stall under an awning, crates and sacks, a bench at the bus stop, people chatting and waiting for the bus, and a child playing. All keep clear of the player's paths and story spots, which the audit and ambient checks test.

**Locs and tams** (`tools/selassie/locs.py`, owner's note November 2026). The locs are now many separate rope-like strands (55 to 78 on each head, 5 to 10 mm thick, a little lumpy like rolled locks, each its own shade, some grey on the elder), rooted over the back, sides and crown and never the forehead, falling under their own weight; side locs end above the shoulders and lean back, longer ones fall down the upper back. Each is kept clear of the scalp and short hair near the head and of the skin and shirt below it, and is weighted from the head through the neck to the upper back, so it follows the head and torso in motion. The tam is a crocheted cap fitted to the real head and hair at every angle: a ribbed band at the brim, a soft body that slouches back and down, and bands of red, gold and green over a dark band, with a few locs falling from under its back edge. Only one of the four Rastafari recipes wears a tam, and many visitors wear hats, caps or nothing on their heads. Photographs of the day (LIFE/Lynn Pelham, the Gleaner, the National Library of Jamaica's "Rasta and his boy have seen the Emperor they Cherish", https://nljdigital.nlj.gov.jm/items/show/774, and its crowd pictures) show bearded Rastafari men, flags, banners and portraits among a crowd in everyday clothes; their captions do not describe hair or headwear, and I could not study the images closely from this machine, so the mix of locs, tams and hats is a respectful general reconstruction, not a count from the photographs.

**The menu picture** (`menu/selassie.jpg`, 1600 x 900 like the Nanny Town card) is rendered from the game itself with `?shot=poster`: the DC-6 at the steps, Haile Selassie I saluting at the top, and the welcome with flags and fronds.

The owner supplied uniform, aircraft and airport photographs in this conversation. They were inspected visually and used as modelling references. No archive photograph or watermark is embedded as a game texture or distributed as an asset.

Every body has raw and low material-separated inspection assets and joined focus, crowd and phone versions. Focus versions preserve the close geometry in two visible draws. Hidden `measureOnly` garment meshes preserve the shared engine's clothing measurements for hand clearance; they do not render. The story uses no seated or kneeling poses, so the shared alternate seated skirt is retained in inspection models and omitted from joined standing render models. `tools/selassie/asset-manifest.json` records builder, finishing and recipe hashes, geometry, packed sizes and source revisions. Normal game builds require no Blender install.

The shared derived `WalkM` showed 20 to 34 cm of sliding on these bodies. This game chooses the shared original `Walk` through ambient behaviour options and calibrates its pace normally. It does not patch or replace the shared walk implementation.

## Checks

Build with `node tools/build.mjs selassie --dev` and serve the repository root. Development URLs include `?test=flow`, `audit`, `held`, `layout`, `follow`, `ambient`, `cloth`, `walkers`, `skin`, `budget`, `heads` and `?auto=1`. `&b=sel_emperor66` selects a body for cloth checks. `?shot=poster`, `terminal`, `emperor`, `face`, `salute`, `people`, `adina` and `memory` are screenshot views (`&at=0.4` picks the moment of the salute); the arrival views pose the cinematic at the salute and frame his real head. These hooks are removed from the production bundle.

**Heads and necks (`?test=heads`, also part of `?test=audit` and `?test=all`).** The owner saw heads "spinning around like a gig" (November 2026). The cause was in the shared ambient system: its head turn was added to the bone every frame, and the mixer only rewrites a bone whose clip value changed; the Idle head track never changes, so each frame's turn piled onto the last. `engine/ambient.js` now starts again from the clip's pose when the mixer left the bone alone (the same guard `gripTick` uses), and a head turns at most 200 degrees a second (a look that swapped sides as the player passed behind someone flicked faster than a person turns). `games/selassie/src/headcheck.js` steps the real frame loop through every place, with the player walking round the family so heads follow, and through the whole arrival, and reports any head more than 80 degrees from the chest, tipped more than 60 degrees, or turning faster than 320 degrees a second, and any camera problem in the cinematic. With the old engine it reports 62 heads; now 0.

**Layout (`?test=layout`)** now also touches every button, link, heading and footer line of an open journal, chapters and credits panel, and a grid over each panel, and fails if anything else is on top, including the journal opened after the arrival cinematic and over a line of narration. The dark bar the owner saw over the journal footer was the empty narration line (`#sub`, above the HUD that holds the journal), left drawn after the cinematic; an empty narration line is no longer drawn and any open panel hides it.
