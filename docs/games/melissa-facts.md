Research date: 14 November 2026 (so "one year later" material, 27 Oct to 4 Nov 2026, is included).
Time zone: all local times are Jamaica time, UTC minus 5, no daylight saving. UTC is given where the source gives UTC.
Confidence key: High = primary source or two agreeing sources. Medium = one good secondary source. Low = unverified or conflicting, do not show to children without a second check.
NOT FOUND = I looked and could not find it.
No em dashes are used in this file. Ranges are written with "to".
No names of private individuals are included. Officials are given by title.

## Top conflicts and what I trust (read this first)

1. **Landfall pressure.** The government statement says "185 mph and 892 mb" at landfall. NHC: 892 mb was the lowest pressure, reached at sea at 7:00 am, about 5 hours BEFORE landfall. At landfall the NHC estimate is 897 mb and 160 kt (about 185 mph). Peak was 165 kt (190 mph) at 7:00 am. Trust [TCR].
2. **Landfall time.** Operational: noon (Met Service Bulletin 52, NHC 1700 UTC). Post-storm analysis: 1725 UTC = 12:25 pm. Trust [TCR] (12:25 pm), say "about 12:30 pm" or "just after midday".
3. **Deaths in Jamaica.** Official count 45 (announced 11 Nov 2025, unchanged in the NHC report of May 2026). Earlier counts 19, 28, 32. In Dec 2025 another 32 deaths were "under investigation" and 13 people were still missing. Leptospirosis deaths (12 linked, 7 confirmed by 10 Dec 2025) are counted separately. No later official change found (NOT FOUND for any change after Dec 2025).
4. **Cost.** Three different figures are in circulation. (a) Government early estimate US$6 to 7 billion, 28 to 32% of GDP. (b) World Bank and IDB GRADE, 19 Nov 2025: US$8.8 billion of PHYSICAL damage = 41% of 2024 GDP. (c) ECLAC/IDB/Government DaLA, Feb 2026, presented by the Planning Institute 3 Mar 2026: US$12.2 billion (J$1.953 trillion) for damage plus losses plus additional costs = 56.7% of 2024 GDP. **The NHC report wrongly joins (b) and (c)** ("12.2 billion physical damage = 41% of GDP"). Use (b) and (c) as described here, not the NHC sentence.
5. **Shelter numbers.** Pre-storm ODPEM said 881 shelters on standby; only 972 people were in 76 shelters on the morning of 27 Oct. After landfall: about 15,000 in shelters at the peak (government and OCHA, 29 Oct) versus 7,208 in 513 shelters (NEOC table in [DALA]). Both are official; they are different counts. Say "thousands".
6. **Structures damaged.** 116,000 "severely or catastrophically damaged" buildings (government, 4 Nov 2025); about 146,000 buildings with major to severe damage (ODPEM, 13 Nov); 156,000 dwellings including 24,000 total losses (early ODPEM); 85,127 houses with some damage or destroyed (DaLA, Jan 2026); more than 118,000 households assessed as needing help (Prime Minister, 27 Oct 2026). Different definitions and dates. Use "tens of thousands of homes" or quote one figure with its source and date.
7. **Gilbert category at Jamaica.** NHC at the time and for decades: Category 3 (127 mph, 960 mb). NHC's current best track: 115 kt (132 mph), which is Category 4, at landfall. JIS, UWI and Wikipedia: Category 3. One Observer feature says Category 5 and 175 mph, which is wrong. Trust NHC: say "a Category 3 hurricane by the original report, upgraded to a low Category 4 in later NHC reanalysis".
8. **Gilbert lowest pressure (888 mb).** It was NOT at Jamaica. It was measured about a day after the eye left Jamaica, in the northwest Caribbean (near 19.5N 83.3W), at 2152 UTC 13 Sep = 4:52 pm Jamaica time on Tuesday 13 Sep. UWI and CARICOM pages that tie 888 mb to the eye leaving western Jamaica are wrong. At Jamaica the pressure was 960 mb (965 mb measured in the eye at Kingston).
9. **Gilbert deaths.** Jamaica: 45 (NHC prelim report, NHC memo, JIS, CSM). Other counts: 49 (EM-DAT, used in DaLA), 50 to 56 (various newspaper pieces). Total: 318 (NHC prelim) or 319 (NHC memo, 1991). Others 341, 433 are less reliable. Trust NHC.
10. **Gilbert people sheltering.** JIS: 800,000 "sought shelter". NHC memo and Jamaican Embassy (1988): about 500,000 homeless. Government estimate in Sept 1988: about 150,000 in organised shelters. Possibly the 800,000 comes from the "total affected" figure (810,000 in EM-DAT, cited in DaLA); that is my inference, not stated. Use "hundreds of thousands lost their homes or roofs".
11. **Gilbert cost for Jamaica.** US$818 million (UN disaster agency provisional, 1988), US$1.0 to 1.5 billion (engineer's paper), "nearly US$2 billion" (NHC prelim), about US$2.5 billion in 2023-24 dollars (EM-DAT via DaLA), US$4 billion (hurricanescience.org, Observer). Do not compare with Melissa without adjusting for inflation.
12. **Tourism recovery one year on.** Conflicting: about 20 hotels still closed (AP-based reports, early Oct 2026), 92% of rooms operational (trade report), about 70% of affected room inventory back in Sept 2026 (officials via a US local paper). Treat as Low until checked against the Jamaica Tourist Board.

| What a hurricane is | A tropical cyclone is a rotating low-pressure weather system with organised thunderstorms. When its top sustained winds reach 74 mph or more it is called a hurricane. ## Suggested use for the game (short)

- Say "Category 5, about 185 mph at landfall, just after midday on Tuesday 28 October 2025, near New Hope, Westmoreland". Do not say "892 mb at landfall".
- Say "45 people died in Jamaica (official count)" and avoid parish tallies in child-facing text unless wanted; keep a note that more cases were still being checked at the time.
- Use shelter and cost figures only with their source and date; prefer the DaLA for cost (US$12.2 billion in damage, losses and extra costs, 56.7% of 2024 GDP) and the World Bank for "US$8.8 billion of damage to buildings and property".
- For Gilbert say "a Category 3 hurricane by the original report; the eye passed over Kingston around midday on Monday 12 September 1988; 45 people died in Jamaica; hundreds of thousands lost homes or roofs".
