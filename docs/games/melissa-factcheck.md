# Fact-check: storm history in the Melissa game (Charlie 1951 and the older storms)

Checked 14 to 15 November 2026. Repo was read only; nothing was edited. All times are Jamaica time (UTC minus 5, which also applies to 1951) unless marked UTC. No em dashes are used in this report or in any suggested wording.

Verdict key: CONFIRMED / CORRECT TO X / SOURCES CONFLICT / COULD NOT VERIFY. Confidence: High, Medium, Low.

---

## 1. Summary: what must change, in priority order

### MUST CHANGE (wrong, or not supported by any source I found)

1. **"Jamaica's deadliest natural disaster of the 1900s" is wrong.** The 14 January 1907 Kingston earthquake killed about 800 to 1,200 people (UWI page says 1,200; common figure about 1,000). Charlie killed about 152 to 154. Even Charlie's own 1951 Gleaner report called Kingston's blow "the heaviest since the earthquake and fire of 1907". The Gleaner (29 Oct 2025) did write "deadliest natural disaster of the 20th century", so our line quotes the Gleaner correctly, but the Gleaner is wrong. Where: JOURNAL `charlie` simple line 4; JOURNAL `storms` deeper "1951" line; docs/games/melissa.md table row 1951.
   - Replace with: **"It was the deadliest hurricane to hit Jamaica in the 1900s."** (True: 1912 about 100, 1903 65 to 90, 1917 57, Charlie 152 to 154.)

2. **Charlie's wind "about 110 miles an hour" is the wrong number for the storm.** NHC best track gives 110 **knots** (about 125 mph) at landfall; the Gleaner of 20 Aug 1951 said "125 mph"; Wikipedia says 125 mph at landfall. The "110 mph" appears to be a Kingston reading (Norton 1952, seen only through Wikipedia) or a knots/mph mix-up. Where: JOURNAL `charlie` deeper line 1.
   - Replace with: **"Charlie was a high Category 3 hurricane. Its winds were about 125 miles an hour near the centre. Kingston had about 17 inches of rain."**

3. **1880 line is wrong in two ways.** Our text: "a hurricane hurt crops and flooded Kingston Harbour. The Gleaner says five people drowned." No source says the harbour was flooded (they say crops, ships and wharves were damaged; the eye passed over Kingston; of 45 ships in harbour only 2 undamaged; most wharves destroyed). "Five drowned" is only part of the toll: the National Library of Jamaica (citing the Jamaica Weather Report of 1915) says 5 drowned and 25 died in fallen houses, and the NHC-based list used by Wikipedia says 30 deaths in Jamaica. Where: JOURNAL `storms` deeper "1880"; docs/games/melissa.md row 1880.
   - Replace with: **"1880: on 18 and 19 August a hurricane crossed the east of the island, right over Kingston. It damaged crops, ships and wharves. About 30 people died."**

4. **Three Charlie details rest on one weak source or are loosely worded** (JOURNAL `charlie` deeper):
   - "About 70 to 80 per cent of the banana crop was destroyed": only found in Wikipedia citing a US newspaper of 21 Aug 1951. Jamaican sources say only "extensive damage" to bananas and coconuts. COULD NOT VERIFY the percentage.
   - "the airport at Palisadoes lost a hangar": the 1951 Gleaner says "Palisadoes Airport was levelled" / installations destroyed. "Lost a hangar" comes from a US wire story. Say "badly damaged".
   - "Hurricane Housing Organization ... joined the Department of Housing in 1956 (one source)": still one source (Sunday Gleaner 31 May 1969, via Wikipedia). I could not verify the name or the 1956 date. What is solidly documented: a hurricane housing programme (UK Parliament Hansard, 1951 to 1955), and Belrock built in 1952 for people who lost homes (Jamaica Information Service, 2022).
   - Replace the three with: **"Much of the banana and coconut crops was destroyed, and the airport at Palisadoes was badly damaged."** and **"After Charlie, the government began a hurricane housing programme, helped by grants and loans from Britain. New communities, such as Belrock in 1952, were built for families who lost their homes."**

5. **Death toll wording: "about 150" is slightly low.** Sources: 152 (Norton 1952, via Wikipedia and a 2024 Gleaner piece), 154 (National Library of Jamaica, UWI, 2004 Gleaner), "more than 150" (Gleaner 17 Aug 2026). Safe wording: **"more than 150"**. Where: JOURNAL `charlie` simple; JOURNAL `storms` simple; quiz line "About 150 people died". The deeper line "about 152 in Jamaica" is fine, or say "152 to 154".

6. **Homeless count is a real conflict; keep the hedge.** 25,000 homeless (Norton via Wikipedia; Gleaner 2004 with a breakdown of 12,000+ Corporate Area, about 10,000 St Thomas, about 3,000 elsewhere) versus "over 9,000 homeless" (National Library of Jamaica and UWI) versus "over 9,000 board and nog houses destroyed" (Gleaner commentary, Dec 2025). Likely reconciliation (my inference only): about 9,000 houses, about 25,000 people. Safe wording: **"About 25,000 people lost their homes."** (say "people", not just "about 25,000") or **"Many thousands of people lost their homes."**

### SHOULD CHANGE (small)

7. Gilbert: "Gilbert's lowest pressure came a day later, near Mexico" (JOURNAL `gilbert` deeper). The 888 mb low was over the north-west Caribbean Sea (about 19.7N 83.8W, 0000 UTC 14 Sep), roughly 200 miles east of the Yucatan coast. It then hit Cozumel and the Yucatan as Category 5. Replace with **"Gilbert's lowest pressure came a day later, over the Caribbean Sea west of Jamaica, where it grew into a Category 5 storm. It then hit Mexico."**
8. Gilbert: "some places waited three months for light". The only source (Jamaica Information Service, 2023) names one community (Over River, St James). Say **"one village waited three months for light."**
9. 1903 cost: our docs say "Gleaner says US$2.5 million". The Gleaner (2021) printed pounds, not dollars (GBP 2,500,000); the Gleaner (2025) printed US$2,500,000; the National Library of Jamaica says damage assessed at GBP 125,000 with GBP 50,000 in relief loans; the New York Times (via Wikipedia) said US$10 million. Keep "no cost" in the game (correct choice). Fix the docs row.
10. docs/games/melissa-facts.md row "First before it": "UWI lists about 150 deaths for Charlie; Low" can now be High: UWI and National Library of Jamaica say 154 confirmed (57 in St Thomas, 54 in the Corporate Area), about 2,000 injured.
11. Landfall hour: docs say "about 10 pm". Best track puts landfall at 0300 UTC = 10:00 pm. The 1951 Gleaner says wind rose about 9:15 pm and the hurricane arrived about 9:30 pm; the worst was the first 45 minutes. The game only says "night", which is fine. If a time is ever stated use **"late in the evening"** or "about 10 pm (best estimate)".
12. docs/games/melissa.md: 1912 row could add "about 100 died, about 40 of them in Montego Bay; the west was hit (Negril, Lucea, Savanna-la-Mar, Montego Bay)". Not required.

### CONFIRMED (no change needed)

Charlie: night of Friday 17 Aug 1951; came in just south of Kingston; Category 3 ("high"); first headline "110 dead"; about 17 inches of rain in Kingston (Medium); Morant Bay about four-fifths destroyed and Port Royal "laid flat" (now checked, see C9); 37 years before Gilbert; "Charlie killed about three times as many as Gilbert".
Older storms: 1780 date, 20-foot surge, Savanna-la-Mar destroyed, "no number" (the sources really do range from 200 or 300 to over 1,000 for Jamaica); 1903 date, Category 3, about 120 mph, near Morant Point, 65 to 90 dead, banana crop wiped out; 1912 date, slow, a week of rain "as much as three feet"; 1980 Allen about 25 miles away, no landfall, eight dead.
Gilbert and Melissa headline numbers: confirmed (see section 4).

## 3. The older storms

### 3a. 1780, 3 October, the Savanna-la-Mar hurricane

Note on naming: the "Great Hurricane of 1780" in the history books is a **different** storm (10 to 16 Oct 1780, Barbados, Martinique, St Eustatius, more than 20,000 dead). The Savanna-la-Mar hurricane is the earlier one. Our game never uses the name "Great Hurricane" (I searched), which is correct. Do not add it.
