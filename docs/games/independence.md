## Idea

Independence told through one fictional Kingston family, as Selassie 1966 and Windrush are. The player lives toward the night of 5 to 6 August 1962 and then past it. Real leaders appear, but the child's eye stays at the centre. Audience: all ages, no violence in the main story.

The family (all fictional, said so in their journal pages):

- **Nell**, about 10, the player.
- **Grandma Cissie**, who remembers 1938.
- **Mum**, who sews, and **Uncle Roy**, who works at a print shop and paints signs.
- Neighbours chosen later. No one is a real person's relative.

## Play and feel (owner, 24 November 2026)

"We need cinematic, game play, interactive story, not too much busy work, but engaging fun, with memorable visual scenes." This sets the shape of every chapter (rules 13 and 14):

- **Cinematic first.** Each chapter has one memorable scene the player remembers by picture, played as a directed close-up with black bars, a gliding camera, sound and short story lines (`engine/lookshot.js`). The scene is something to watch and be part of, not a card.
- **Interactive, not chores.** Two or three meaningful things to do per chapter, each with a visible result in the world and a payoff in the story. Cut any fetch, carry or talk step that is only a button press. Choices change Nell's memory of the day, not the record.
- **Fun.** Short playful moments with real skill or surprise: painting the flag colours onto cloth and seeing the bunting go up along the street, running a message through a crowd, finding the best spot to see, singing the anthem line by line with the school.
- **Memorable pictures** (each modelled on sources, rule 3): the street strung with homemade bunting; the stadium at night with lights and a huge crowd; the flag coming down and the new one rising at midnight; the lane at dawn with paper flags in the gutter; a school yard today.

## Chapters (draft, six main plus one memory)

1. **The paper on the gate** (Kingston yard, July 1962). The family hears the flag will change. Neighbours disagree gently. Carry the newspaper to Grandma. Her talk opens a playable memory.
   - *Memory, 1938* (short, playable, as Selassie's 1930 memory): a Kingston street in the labour unrest, seen from a safe place, in the owner's gentle-history style. No fighting shown. Verify what to show with `docs/CONTENT.md` rule 4.
2. **The long road** (Kingston, years in short story cards). 1944 vote, the Federation of the West Indies and the 1961 referendum, told as Grandma's and Roy's talk plus a close-up look at a real object (rule 13), such as a ballot paper or a printed poster. Manley and Bustamante can appear here through the reported record, see "How the leaders appear".
3. **Painting the colours** (Roy's shop). Make the flag and bunting: choose colours, hold a brush, hang the cloth. The real flag and its design history are verified first. The anthem is learned at school.
4. **The stadium, 5 August** (National Stadium, evening). The crowd, the programme, the leaders, the Governor-General, the royal representative. Verify who was present and what was done.
5. **Midnight** (stadium). The Union Jack comes down and the new flag goes up. A directed close-up with black bars and short story lines (`engine/lookshot.js`), sound, no invented words for the flag moment.
6. **The morning after, and today** (the lane at dawn, then a school yard now). Ordinary Saturday and Monday, hopes and worries, then a present-day scene that ends on the anthem and a child.

Chapter 2 is the one most likely to feel like a history lesson. Rule 14 applies: every task earns its place. Cut or merge any step that is only a button press.

## How the leaders appear

Rule 3 allows real people to say **imagined everyday words** that complete a scene if they fit the facts and what is known of the person, and the journal page says so. Garvey's game keeps to sourced words only. For two sitting politicians in a still-argued history, this game takes the careful middle:

- **Real words only for anything political**: a sourced line, quoted exactly, with its source on the journal page. No invented speeches, promises, arguments or "famous quotes".
- **Imagined words only for plain everyday moments** (a greeting, a thank-you, "Good evening"), kept short, and every one marked as imagined on the journal page.
- **Both leaders are shown fairly and in equal measure.** The two parties disagreed, and the game says that plainly and gently. No hero, no villain. Neither is shown winning an argument the sources do not show.
- **Rule 3 on looks**: each is modelled on photographs of that very person and period (face, build, clothes, hair), with the sources listed here. Until then they are stand-ins and the doc says so. Bodies follow `docs/PEOPLE.md` and rule 15's full check.
- The Governor-General and the royal representative appear only as far as photographs and accounts support, with no invented words.
- Open question for the owner: should the leaders speak at all in the stadium scene, or only be seen and heard in sourced radio or newspaper words? Safest default is sourced words only.

## Rules specific to this game (proposed, owner to confirm)

1. Real politicians: sourced words for politics, imagined words only for everyday moments, journal says which (see above).
2. Both parties fairly shown. The history is not a campaign.
3. The independence story is joyful **and** honest: hopes and worries, emigration (Windrush), unfinished business. Told gently (rule 4).
4. Everything modelled on sources: the flag, stadium, uniforms, buses, cars, bunting, radio. Anything still a stand-in is listed here.
5. The player never takes a side in the real politics; choices change Nell's memory, not the record (as in Selassie).
