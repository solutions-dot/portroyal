## The rule for this game

A real woman, Annie Palmer, is accused by the legend of murders the records do not support. The game never shows the legend as fact. Every legend scene is marked LEGEND on screen and told as shadow figures, and the record chapters say plainly what the papers show. Nobody real has invented words: the documents are quoted.

## Legend against record

| The legend says | First in print | The record says |
|---|---|---|
| A lady owner was strangled in her bed and kept iron collars for slaves | Hope Waddell, 1863, p. 53. It is about **Palmyra**, the next estate, and names nobody | Geoffrey Yates (about 1965) found no inquest and no newspaper report |
| "Mrs Ann Palmer" of Rose Hall killed three husbands, tortured slaves and was killed by them | James Castello's pamphlet, Falmouth Post, 1868. No Haiti, voodoo, Takoo or "White Witch" in it | The church monument it points to is Rosa Palmer's |
| Haiti, a voodoo nurse, Irish parents, "the White Witch", Robert Rutherford, Millicent, Takoo, death in the 1831 rebellion | Herbert de Lisser's novel, 1929 | Born 1802 into Hanover planter families. No Takoo or Millicent among the 208 people recorded at Rose Hall, 1817 to 1832 |
| Three husbands | 1868 | One: John Rose Palmer |
| Killed in 1831 | 1929 | Sold her rights in 1830 for 200 pounds, died 1846 |
| A tomb at Rose Hall | 1868 | Buried in Montego Bay churchyard, 9 July 1846. "No tombstone has survived" (Yates) |
| Arsenic, boiling oil, the tomb's crosses, the face in the mirror | Modern tour retellings only | No record |

## Documented lives

- **Annie Mary Paterson**: born autumn 1802, only child of John Paterson of The Baulk, near Lucea, and Juliana Brown. Married John Rose Palmer at Mount Pleasant, St James, 28 March 1820. No children, never remarried. Sold "whatever rights she may have had" in Rose Hall and Palmyra in 1830. Died 1846 at Bonavista, near Anchovy. She did hold people in slavery: four in 1829, eight in 1833.
- **John Rose Palmer**: baptised in Carew, Pembrokeshire, 1785; inherited the mortgaged estates in 1818; died at Rose Hall, November 1827, aged 42, with debts of about 6,000 pounds. Cause of death not found. Obituary: "His intrinsic worth, kind heart, and generous disposition obtained him the esteem of all his acquaintance."
- **Rosa Palmer** (born Kelly, about 1718, died 1 May 1790), the first mistress of Rose Hall. Four husbands: Henry Fanning, George Ash, Norwood Witter, John Palmer. Her monument by John Bacon (1794) is in St James Parish Church, Montego Bay. It begins: "Near this place are deposited the Remains of Mrs Rosa Palmer, who died on the first day of May 1790. Her manners were open, cheerful and agreeable". Waddell in 1830 saw in the empty house "three or four full-size pictures of the husbands of the last proprietress". The legend seems to have mixed the two women.

## The people enslaved at Rose Hall (Celia Naylor, "Unsilencing Slavery", 2022; the Rose Hall Journal, Jamaica Archives 1B/26)

208 named people, 1817 to 1832; over 90 died in fourteen years. Clothing lists give osnaburg and peniston, no shoes. Named in the journal:

- **Celia** (Catherine Lewis), born about 1772, "Grasscutter", "weakly", a great-grandmother.
- **Dorinda** (Rosy Mowatt), "Hospital Attendant and Midwife", nine children, four dead before age five. Journal, 21 April 1830: "Dorrinda the midwife being orderd to the field for general neglect in the performance of her duty thot proper to abscond."
- **Cecelia**, African-born about 1769, "Attending Young Children". **Mary James**, "G.H. Attendant". **George Stennet**, "Head Driver". **Aaron**, African-born, "Head Penkeeper". **Adonis**, "Head Cooper".
- **Hercules** ran away at least 26 times, **Doshy** 16. **Archey** was "sent to Falmouth Workhouse for punishment" in 1823 and "transported" in 1826, aged 20.
- Naylor: of the legend's events, "All of them are fabricated". The journal says nothing of what happened at Rose Hall in the rebellion.

## Dates for the place

- Great house: Hakewill (1825) says built "about fifty years since" by John Palmer for 30,000 pounds; others say George Ash in the 1750s. Sources disagree: say so.
- Hakewill, about 1820: "A double flight of stone steps leads to an open portico", eating-room left, drawing-room right, a mahogany staircase, "a very extensive sea view".
- August 1830: Waddell preached in "the old unoccupied Great House of Rose Hall", "unoccupied, save by rats, bats, and owls".
- 27 December 1831: the rebellion began at Kensington, St James. Samuel Sharpe was hanged 23 May 1832. Full freedom: 1 August 1838.
- In ruins by 1915; restored by John Rollins, finished 1971. "Annie's bedroom" and the "dungeon" have no period source.

## Words to take care with

"Voodoo" is Haitian Vodou and does not belong to Jamaica. Obeah enters the legend in 1895, voodoo in 1929. Scholars say the legend makes one white woman a scapegoat and forgets 208 named people. Yates: "there was no murder, no motive and no evidence"; "The legend throve, the facts disappeared".
