# National Heroes: the images we accept (23 November 2026)

The owner's direction: "we need to model our national heroes to the images we accept as them."

This page lists, for each of Jamaica's seven National Heroes, the images in use, what is known about them, and what the owner has to accept before a body or face is built. A face is shaped against the one image the owner accepts, as Marcus Garvey's was (`docs/STANDARD.md`, "A real person's likeness"; `docs/games/garvey.md`). Record the image and its source in the game's doc and check every later change against it. Photographs and banknote portraits are reference only, never textures. Skin stays in rule 9's range and is lightened in game.

**The honest rule for the journal:** every hero's page says what kind of picture the face comes from: a photograph of the person, a portrait, or an artist's picture because none was made in their lifetime.

## What this changes in Morant Bay, 1865

The first plan kept Bogle a far figure with no face, because no verified likeness exists. The owner has decided the heroes follow the accepted images, so:
- Bogle and Gordon get faces shaped against the accepted image, recorded here and in `docs/games/morant-bay.md`.
- They still have **no invented words** (the owner's rule for real people, and the Commission records only witnesses' reports). The real letters appear on screen as documents.
- Bogle's journal page says: no picture made in his lifetime is certain; the face is the photograph Jamaica has used since 1959; some doubt it shows him; the statue at Morant Bay shows an artist's idea.
- Clothes follow the photograph for Bogle (a dark three-piece suit, a frilled collar) only if the owner accepts it as his dress. The Commission does not describe what he wore.

## Open questions for the owner

1. Accept the 1959 photograph for Bogle? Age the face toward 43?
2. Where does the Gordon portrait come from? Do you have it?
3. Should the Maroon officers and the Governor be modelled on any image? (Governor Eyre has well-known portraits; none is used yet, and Eyre has no invented words.)
4. Permission: the NLJ images may need a permission request before any face goes public, even as a modelling reference. Ask the NLJ and the Jamaica National Heritage Trust before release.
