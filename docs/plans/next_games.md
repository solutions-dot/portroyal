## Pinned (owner: "stick a pin there")

**Xaymaca, 1503 to 1504** (working title, all ages). A Taino child watches Columbus's broken ships beached at St Ann's Bay. JIS says Xaymaca means "land of wood and water".

What was read (Wikipedia's article on the fourth voyage, partly truncated, so not complete):
- Two worm-eaten ships reached St Ann's Bay about 23 or 25 June 1503 (sources differ) and were beached as past repair. 116 men were marooned for about a year.
- July 1503: Diego Mendez and Bartolomeo Fieschi crossed to Hispaniola in dugout canoes with 12 Spaniards and 20 Taino paddlers. They reached Navassa Island in three days, where several paddlers died or fell very ill of thirst, then finished the crossing in a day. The governor, Ovando, arrested them and refused a rescue ship.
- 2 January 1504: 48 men under Francisco and Diego de Porras mutinied and marched east across the island, harming Taino villages.
- February 1504: the Taino cut back the food. Columbus used the lunar eclipse of 29 February 1504 to win help.
- The rescue came about 28 to 29 June 1504 (the date is uncertain).
- Almost everything comes from the Spanish side. There is no Taino account, so the child's thoughts are invented and the journal must say so.

Shape proposed: the child's older cousin is one of the twenty paddlers. The question running through the game is "Will the village keep feeding the strangers, and will the cousin come home?" Chapters: ships on the bay (cinematic of the beaching), bread for bells, the paddlers (cinematic dawn departure), the night runner (the mutineers marching east), the red moon (the big cinematic), when the ships left and "Back in 2026". Open: whether the cousin's fate is left open (the record names no paddler). Nothing sacred is shown (as Nanny Town). A Taino-heritage reviewer before anything goes live. Ships, houses and dress need sources.
