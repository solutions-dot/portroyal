      Where the history comes from

      • The Jamaica Gleaner: "1904 Bog Walk disaster relived" (2 July 2021) and "33 die in Bog Walk tube incident" (6 July 2022), which retell the Gleaner's report of 28 June 1904
      • Rebecca Tortello, "Bog Walk Tube", Pieces of the Past (Jamaica Gleaner)
      • Wikipedia, "Bog Walk" (citing Brian Hudson, Waterfalls of Jamaica, 2003) and "Rio Cobre Dam"
      • jamaicans.com, "See What's Left of the Bog Walk Tube" (2016), for the ruins

      The original Gleaner of 28 June 1904 has not been read yet, so some facts still need checking: the date, the spelling of names, and the exact headline.

      You, Rudy, Old Zeke, Miss Ivy, Bass Henry, the gang, Mr Bertie, the people of Kingston in 1899, the man from the Gleaner, Miss Grant and Kemar are fictional. Mr Douparrouzel, Dr Hammond, the two ministers, Mr Haggart and Mr Osborne-Smith are real. The 33 men who died were real, and are not named in this game.

      The pipe, the station, the valve, the trams and the clothes are our best guess from general knowledge. We have not found photographs or plans to copy.
