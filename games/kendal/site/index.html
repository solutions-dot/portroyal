Jamaica · 1 September 1957

Kendal 1957 The Excursion Train

A long train takes a church group from Kingston to the sea. On the way home that night, it crashes near Kendal. It is the worst train crash in Jamaica's history.

First, learn how people travelled before trains, and how the railway came to Jamaica. This is a sad story, told gently. It is also a story about helping.

Where the history comes from

  • The Jamaica National Heritage Trust, Kendal Railway Tragedy site page
  • The Jamaica Gleaner, on the Kendal crash (1957 report pages, a 2017 anniversary report, and later articles)
  • The Jamaica Observer, on the survivors, the commemoration and the 2025 monument
  • The National Library of Jamaica, Lives Derailed: On the 60th Anniversary of the Kendal Train Crash
  • Peter Espeut, Remembering the Kendal crash (Gleaner, 2026), for the findings of the Commission of Enquiry
  • Wikipedia, Rail transport in Jamaica and Kingston to Montego Bay railway, for dates and stations (to be checked against the railway's own records)
  • A newspaper account of the opening of the Jamaica Railway on 21 November 1845, as reprinted by the Historic Jamaica blog

You, Gran, Cousin Lloyd, Mass Ezra, Mass Dudley, the people on the road, at the stations, on the train and at Kendal are fictional. The Kendal crash, the railway and the helpers of Kendal are real. Colours, clothes and the look of the carriages are our best guess from general knowledge. We have not found photographs to copy.
