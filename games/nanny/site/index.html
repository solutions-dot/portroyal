Nanny Town 1734 The Narrow Path

High in the Blue Mountains, people who escaped slavery built a free town. Its leader was Nanny. Soldiers came up the one narrow path, again and again.

Walk there by night. Learn the horn. Stand watch. A story of courage, told gently.

Where the history comes from

  • The Jamaica National Heritage Trust, site pages for Nanny Town and Moore Town, and its facts on the National Heroes
  • Bryan Edwards, The History, Civil and Commercial, of the British Colonies in the West Indies (first printed 1793), for the 1734 attack. He was a planter and wrote almost 60 years later.
  • Philip Thicknesse, Memoirs and Anecdotes (1788), and R. C. Dallas, The History of the Maroons (1803), for horns, sentries, trenches and food at a Windward town in 1739
  • The docket of the land patent of 5 August 1740 (UK National Archives, CO 137/28), read in a transcription
  • The National Library of Jamaica, history notes on the Maroons and on Portland, for the treaties of 1739
  • UNESCO, on the Blue and John Crow Mountains (2015) and the Maroon heritage of Moore Town (2008)
  • The Jamaica Information Service, the Gleaner and the Observer, on Nanny as a National Hero and on the 500 dollar note
  • Edward Long, The History of Jamaica (1774), for day names

You, Mama, Quamin, Auntie Mimba, Cubbenah, Beneba and Phibba are fictional. So are the night walk, the fight on the path and every word people say. Nanny, Nanny Town, the attacks, the treaties and the 1740 paper are real.

Nanny does not speak in this game, because nobody wrote down her words. Nobody knows what she looked like, so she is only seen from far away. Maroon ceremonies are private and are not shown.

The huts, the clothes, the plants and the horn calls are our best guess. This game has not yet been reviewed by the Moore Town Maroon Council. We would like it to be.
